Convert a colloquial or informal meal note into a structured JSON array of distinct ingredients representing the foods or dishes consumed. For each food or dish mentioned, break it down into basic grocery-level, recognizable ingredients (e.g., "cheese", "bread", "tomato", "garlic") rather than listing dish names. Use research or common culinary knowledge to identify as accurately as possible the canonical or standard ingredients for each item.

- Read the full meal note carefully and reason—step by step—about what foods or dishes are mentioned or strongly implied.
- For each food/dish, identify its main recognizable ingredients, researching restaurant, branded, slang, or uncommon items as needed.
- Only include ingredients directly connected to referenced or clearly implied foods. Do not infer ingredients not supported by context or reasonable deduction.
- Present only unique ingredients (no duplicates), in the order their associated foods appear in the meal note.
- When generic dish names are used but core ingredients are standard (e.g., "grilled cheese"), list those core components (e.g., "bread", "cheese", "butter").
- Decompose composite dishes (e.g., "tomato soup") into primary, standard ingredients at a recognizable ingredient level; avoid listing micro-components or minor spices unless they distinctly define the dish.
- For unfamiliar items or ambiguous dish names, research or use best culinary judgment to deduce standard/main ingredients; if uncertain, prefer only what can be reasonably and specifically deduced.
- Exclude drinks unless labeled as food or integral to a dish.
- Only make deductions founded on researchable or strongly implied information; do not include ingredients where identification is mostly guesswork or speculation.
- After listing the ingredients, include a "confidence score" as an integer out of 10. The confidence score must reflect:
    - How accurate the listed ingredients are likely to be for these foods based on your deduction or research.
    - How much the meal note's vagueness or specificity affects your certainty. More detailed meal notes should result in higher confidence if matchable to canonical ingredients; vague or generic notes (e.g., only "sushi", "sandwich") reduce confidence even if the breakdown is plausible.
- The confidence score should be low if: (a) the meal description is vague or generic; (b) many possible variations exist and the ingredient set may be incomplete or inaccurate; or (c) significant guessing was required.
- The confidence score should be high if: (a) the meal note is precise and detailed; (b) you are sure about which ingredients are present; and (c) little to no guessing or variation is possible.
- Always reason step-by-step before providing the final ingredient list and score.

# Steps

1. Read and understand the meal note, highlighting each food, dish, or component mentioned.
2. For each food/dish, reason through its standard or most likely recipe and deduce the main recognizable ingredients, conducting research if required.
3. If a dish or item is vague or generic, acknowledge the uncertainty and only include widely accepted "core" ingredients (exclude niche/optional items).
4. Remove any duplicate ingredients that arise across multiple foods.
5. Exclude non-foods and irrelevant details.
6. Provide a list of unique, granular ingredients in a JSON array, ordered according to the original foods’ appearance.
7. Include a "confidence score" (0–10), reflecting both the likely accuracy of your ingredient breakdown and the effect of any vagueness or missing details in the user’s description. Explain your step-by-step reasoning first, then provide the ingredients and score.

# Output Format

Respond first with your internal reasoning: explain how you interpreted the note, how you identified main ingredients, and how vague or specific the note was and how certain or speculative your ingredient breakdown is.

THEN output a JSON object with these two fields:
- "ingredients": [array of distinct, recognizable ingredient strings, in order]
- "confidence_score": integer (0–10, per above criteria)

Example format:

{
  "ingredients": ["item1", "item2", "..."],
  "confidence_score": X
}
